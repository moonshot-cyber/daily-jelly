## Best overall: BB LAB Pomegranate Collagen S

£18–24 for 14 sticks · Marine (fish) collagen · ~1,000mg per stick

The Olive Young-famous format — the closest thing to "the original" Korean jelly stick. Fish collagen and pomegranate concentrate amounts are clearly labelled (~1,000mg fish collagen + 18,600mg pomegranate concentrate per stick), which is more transparency than most Korean imports offer. No UK high-street stockist, so it's an international checkout via YesStyle or Stylevana either way.

The 1,000mg dose is on the lower end compared to Cloo9 or NOW Foods, but BB LAB is what started the format's popularity — it's the one you'll see in every Korean beauty haul video.

## Best for UK retailer choice: FOODOLOGY Collagen Centella Jelly

£14–22 for 10 sticks · Collagen peptides + Centella Asiatica

Stocked across several Korean-beauty resellers, so easier to find in stock than most. The standout ingredient alongside the collagen is Centella Asiatica, a well-known K-beauty skin-soothing botanical. We couldn't confirm an exact mg collagen dose from the listing — check the pack when it arrives.

Worth noting: FOODOLOGY also makes a separate "Cutting Jelly" product, which is a fibre supplement for weight management, not collagen. Make sure you're ordering the Collagen Centella version.

## Best high-dose option: Cloo9 Collagen Jelly

£64 for 28 sticks · Marine collagen (hydrolysed, Type I) · 5,000mg per stick

The highest labelled dose on this list by a wide margin — 5,000mg of marine collagen per stick, roughly double the next-highest option (NOW Foods at 2,500mg). Sold direct from Cloo9's own site in GBP with UK delivery, no international checkout needed. Available in Peach or Cherry.

It's also the most expensive per stick at ~£2.29, though if you're comparing by dose it's actually the cheapest per 1,000mg of collagen. ## Best vegan: YIXO Vegan Collagen Jelly

£26 for 21 jellies · Plant-based collagen-support blend

UK-based and sold direct, so the simplest delivery of any option here alongside the Amazon pick. Important to understand: this is a plant-based collagen-support blend (Vitamin C + Hyaluronic Acid), not real collagen — there's no plant source of animal collagen, and no vegan product can contain actual collagen. That said, it's the only genuinely vegan option on this list.

The dosing is different from every other product here: you eat 3 jellies a day (not 1), making the real daily cost ~£3.71, not the £1.24 per jelly the headline maths might suggest. ## Best from a brand you already trust: NOW Foods Collagen Jelly Beauty Complex

£16.03 for 10 sticks · Bovine collagen (VERISOL) · 2,500mg per stick

NOW Foods is an established supplement brand most UK shoppers already recognise, and this is the only product on our list using VERISOL — the specific, patented bovine collagen peptide behind the most-studied trial data. VERISOL (Bioactive Collagen Peptides, 2.5g daily) was used in the Proksch et al. 2014 trial that most collagen evidence reviews cite. Bought from Welzo, a UK pharmacy-style retailer.

The collagen here is bovine (Types I and III), not marine — worth noting if you have a preference or if fish is a concern. Available in Sweet Orange or Sweet Plum.

## Best if you're already on Amazon: HAMCHOROK Rg+ Pomegranate Collagen Jelly Stick

~£36–42 for 30 sticks · Marine (fish) collagen · Dose not stated on listing

The biggest pack on this list at 30 sticks — a full month at one a day — dispatched by Amazon for Prime-speed UK delivery and Amazon's own returns process. Contains pomegranate concentrate, fermented black ginseng concentrate, and low-molecular-weight fish collagen, though the listing doesn't state an exact mg dose.

A note on the Amazon listing itself: it leans on unproven claims (anti-ageing, immunity) that go beyond what's authorised in the UK. We don't repeat those — go by the ingredients, not the title copy.

## A note on collagen and skin claims

If you're looking for the "best collagen jelly stick for face" or skin specifically, it's worth knowing that UK and EU rules permit only one authorised health claim connecting a nutrient to collagen: **Vitamin C contributes to normal collagen formation for the normal function of skin.** Claims like "boosts collagen," "reduces wrinkles," or "restores elasticity" go beyond what's currently authorised. The presence of Vitamin C at a meaningful dose is the one ingredient with a legally backed connection to skin — everything else is marketing.

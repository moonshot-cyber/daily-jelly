Four of the six collagen jelly sticks we track — [BB LAB](/where-to-buy), [FOODOLOGY](/where-to-buy), [Cloo9](/where-to-buy), and [HAMCHOROK](/where-to-buy) — use fish collagen. Fish is one of the UK's 14 legally mandated food allergens, meaning it has to be named and visually emphasised on the label wherever it's an ingredient. Checking all four listings directly, only one of them does that clearly. That's the real, checkable finding behind this piece — not a scare story, just what's actually on each label.

## Is fish legally required to be flagged as an allergen?

Yes. Fish is one of the 14 allergens UK food businesses must declare under retained EU food information law, enforced via the Food Standards Agency — when it's present as an ingredient, it has to be named and emphasised (bold, capitals, or underlined), not just listed alongside everything else. A narrow exemption exists for fish gelatine used purely as a carrier for vitamin preparations or as a fining agent in beer and wine — that doesn't apply here, since fish collagen is a substantial, named ingredient in these products, not a processing aid.

What we found checking the four fish-collagen listings directly: three of them list "Fish Collagen" in the ingredients but don't give it the same bolded treatment as milk, which is flagged on the same listings. We're reporting what's visible on the online listing as it's displayed to a UK shopper — we haven't inspected the physical pack of any of these products, and formatting doesn't always survive the trip from pack to website, so this isn't a claim that the physical labelling itself is non-compliant. It's a reason to read the actual ingredients list yourself rather than relying on a listing's allergen callouts, since they're clearly inconsistent across these products.

## What the allergy research actually says

The evidence here is genuinely mixed, not one-sided. Fish collagen is a documented allergen: studies have found IgE sensitisation to fish collagen in a meaningful minority of fish-allergic patients — one study put it at around 21% — and pooled serum from collagen-sensitised patients showed reactivity across multiple fish species, suggesting real cross-reactivity rather than a reaction limited to one type of fish. The allergen is also heat-stable, meaning cooking or processing doesn't reliably remove the risk the way it can with some other food allergens.

Against that, two separate European studies using actual food challenges — the gold-standard way of confirming a real allergic reaction, rather than just a positive blood or skin test — found collagen to be less clinically significant than parvalbumin, the muscle protein that's the main driver of most fish allergies. In other words: some people with a diagnosed fish allergy tolerate fish collagen fine, and a positive lab test for collagen sensitisation doesn't necessarily mean a real-world reaction. There's no reliable way to predict which applies to you without being tested specifically, which is exactly why this is a question for an allergist rather than something to guess at from a label.

## What a reaction actually looks like

Standard fish-allergy symptoms apply here too: hives, an itchy throat or mouth, swelling of the face or lips, or stomach symptoms like nausea and vomiting, usually within minutes of eating. Severe reactions are less common but can include difficulty breathing, throat swelling, or fainting — a medical emergency. If you or someone else has those severe symptoms after eating a collagen jelly stick, call 999 and say "anaphylaxis"; use an adrenaline auto-injector if one is available and prescribed. For anything milder, or if you're unsure whether a past reaction to fish means you need to avoid fish collagen specifically, that's a conversation for a GP or allergist, not something this page can answer for you individually.

## What you're actually allowed to claim

None of this is medical advice, and we're not offering any. In the UK and EU, the only authorised claim connecting a nutrient to collagen is that **Vitamin C contributes to normal collagen formation for the normal function of skin**. Everything above is documented safety and labelling information, reported as accurately as we can check it — not a diagnosis, not a recommendation for or against any specific product, and not a substitute for an allergist's advice if you have a known fish allergy.

## The honest verdict

If you have a diagnosed fish allergy, treat BB LAB, FOODOLOGY, Cloo9, and HAMCHOROK as a genuine question for your allergist rather than a guess based on what is or isn't bolded on the listing — the research doesn't support a blanket "avoid" or "it's fine" either way. NOW Foods is the one product on our list that's genuinely fish-free (bovine collagen). YIXO's vegan claim is plausible but unverified from the brand's own published ingredients, so check directly with them if a fish-free product specifically is what you need.

A collagen jelly stick is a tear-open, ready-to-eat pouch of soft-set collagen jelly — no water, mixing, or swallowing a pill required, unlike gummies, powders, or capsules. If you've spent any time in a Korean pharmacy, convenience store, or an Olive Young beauty aisle, you'll have seen them: slim foil pouches, usually 20–30g each, sold in boxes of 10 to 30, sitting next to skincare rather than in a "supplements" section. That placement is the whole point. In Korea, these are treated as a beauty product you eat, not a pill you swallow.

## What is a collagen jelly stick, exactly?

A collagen jelly stick is exactly what it sounds like — a stick-shaped sachet filled with a soft, spoonable-but-drinkable jelly. You tear off the top and either squeeze it straight into your mouth or eat it with a small amount left in the pouch, like a fruit puree pouch for adults. No water, no swallowing a tablet, no mixing a powder into a drink.

Typical contents:

- **A collagen source** — usually low-molecular-weight fish (marine) collagen peptides, sometimes paired with a plant-based booster
- **A fruit concentrate** — pomegranate is common, along with peach, mango, and green grape, mostly for flavour but often marketed alongside the collagen story
- **Vitamin C** — added deliberately, because it's the one nutrient with an actual authorised claim tied to collagen (more on that below)
- **A gelling agent** — usually a food-grade thickener that gives the jelly its texture without needing refrigeration

For a deeper look at the collagen sources and how doses compare across real products, see our [ingredients and benefits guide](/guides/collagen-jelly-stick-benefits-and-ingredients/).

## What does a collagen jelly stick taste like?

Most taste like a concentrated fruit puree — pomegranate is the most common flavour across Korean brands, followed by peach and mango. The texture sits somewhere between a thick smoothie and a soft gummy: squeezable from the pouch, but set enough that it holds its shape briefly on a spoon. You don't need to chew it the way you would a gummy, and you don't need to drink it.

The fruit flavouring does most of the heavy lifting. Some sticks have a faint supplement-like note underneath — not unpleasant, but noticeable if you're used to sweets rather than supplements. Flavour quality varies between brands; our [best collagen jelly sticks UK](/guides/best-collagen-jelly-sticks-uk/) roundup notes which flavours each product offers.

## How much collagen is in a jelly stick?

Doses vary more than you might expect. Across the six products we currently track in the UK:

- **Cloo9** — 5,000mg marine collagen per stick (the highest we've found)
- **NOW Foods** — 2,500mg bovine collagen per stick (VERISOL peptides)
- **BB LAB** — ~1,000mg fish collagen per stick
- **FOODOLOGY** and **HAMCHOROK** — collagen peptides listed but no exact mg stated on the listing
- **YIXO** — a plant-based collagen-support blend, not comparable as a dose

There's no standard dose for a jelly stick, and there's no UK-authorised claim linking any specific milligram amount of collagen to a skin benefit. The clinical trials most commonly cited used 2.5–5g daily of specific peptides (see our [evidence roundup](/guides/do-collagen-jelly-sticks-actually-work/) for the detail), but that doesn't mean a jelly stick at those doses will deliver the same result — the trial conditions, peptide types, and study populations all vary.

## Why the format matters more than the ingredients

The collagen itself isn't new — collagen supplements have been sold in the UK for years as gummies, powders, and capsules. What's different is *how* it's taken. Korean "inner beauty" brands treat supplementation as a snacking habit: something you reach for the way you'd reach for a piece of fruit, not something you have to remember to take with a glass of water at breakfast. That's a habit-formation advantage gummies and powders don't have — no prep, no cleanup, shelf-stable, and small enough to carry in a bag or pocket.

## What health claims are allowed for collagen jelly sticks?

UK and EU rules only permit one authorised health claim connecting a nutrient to collagen: **"Vitamin C contributes to normal collagen formation for the normal function of skin."** That's it. Claims like "boosts collagen production," "reduces wrinkles," or "restores elasticity" go beyond what's currently authorised, regardless of what the packaging says in Korean or how the brand markets it overseas. If you're comparing products, the presence of vitamin C at a meaningful dose is the one ingredient with a legally backed reason to be there — everything else is taste, texture, and marketing.

## Where can you buy collagen jelly sticks in the UK?

Because the format hasn't reached UK high-street shelves yet, availability is limited to specialist importers, international K-beauty retailers, and a small number of UK-based direct-to-consumer brands.

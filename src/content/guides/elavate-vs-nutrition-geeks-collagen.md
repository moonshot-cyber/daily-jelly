Elavate and Nutrition Geeks are the two UK collagen powders that come up together most often — and they are very different products at very different price points. One is a multi-ingredient blend with added actives; the other is a single-ingredient bovine powder. This guide sets out what the two labels actually say, side by side, and works out the price per serving and per gram of collagen. **We haven't tested either product.** Everything below comes from each brand's own label and product page, as read on 26 October 2026, plus our own arithmetic.

This is not a verdict on which is better. A label comparison can tell you what you're paying for; it can't tell you what you'll notice.

## What each label says

These two products measure their servings differently, so the table states what each figure is rather than implying a direct match.

| | Elavate (chocolate flavour) | Nutrition Geeks Collagen Glow Up |
|---|---|---|
| **Serving size** | 12g (2 teaspoons) | 14g (2 flat tablespoons) |
| **Collagen per serving** | 8,090mg multi-collagen blend | 12.6g protein (from collagen peptides) |
| **Collagen source** | Bovine (7,900mg), marine (150mg), chicken (40mg) — about 98% bovine | Bovine only — Type I and Type III, grass-fed Brazilian cattle |
| **Collagen type stated?** | No | Type I and Type III (proportions not given) |
| **Vitamin C** | 300mg (liposomal) | None — removed in a reformulation |
| **Hyaluronic acid** | 120mg | None |
| **Biotin** | 5mg (10,000% of reference value) | None |
| **Probiotics** | 50 million CFUs (two strains) | None |
| **Other added ingredients** | Zinc, vitamins B12, D3, K2 | None — single ingredient |
| **Calories per serving** | 39 kcal | 50 kcal |
| **Protein per serving** | 7.9g | 12.6g |
| **Servings per pack** | "20 to 40 depending on how you use it" — pack weight not stated | 30 (420g pack) |
| **Allergens flagged** | Fish (from the marine collagen); soy in vanilla flavour | "Free from common allergens" — no allergen statement on the page |
| **Third-party tested?** | Not mentioned on the page | Yes — label-accuracy test displayed (24 Aug 2026) |

The serving-size difference matters for the collagen comparison. Elavate's 8,090mg is the weight of a multi-collagen blend in a 12g serving. Nutrition Geeks' 12.6g is the protein content of a 14g single-ingredient serving. Neither figure is wrong, but they are not measuring the same thing, and reading them as a straight "who has more" comparison overstates the difference or understates it depending on what you're counting.

## What each one costs

Prices are from each brand's own product page, read 26 October 2026. Both offer one-off and subscription options, with lower prices on bulk deals.

| | Elavate (per serving at 20 servings a bag) | Nutrition Geeks (per serving, 30 a pack) |
|---|---|---|
| **Single pack, one-off** | £2.45 | £0.57 |
| **Single pack, subscription** | £1.95 | £0.51 |
| **Best bulk deal, one-off** | £1.13 (six bags) | £0.47 (six packs) |
| **Best bulk deal, subscription** | £0.98 (nine bags) | £0.42 (six packs) |

Per serving, Nutrition Geeks is roughly a quarter to a third of the Elavate price. But a serving of each contains different amounts of collagen and different numbers of added ingredients, so the per-serving comparison is not the whole picture.

On a per-1,000mg-of-collagen basis:

| | Per 1,000mg of collagen |
|---|---|
| **Elavate, one-off (at 20 servings)** | about £0.30 |
| **Elavate, nine-bag subscription** | about £0.12 |
| **Nutrition Geeks, one-off single** | about £0.04 |
| **Nutrition Geeks, six-pack subscription** | about £0.03 |

That gap is real but narrower than the per-serving gap, because Nutrition Geeks' serving delivers more collagen. It still does not account for the added ingredients in Elavate that Nutrition Geeks doesn't include. Whether those extras — vitamin C, hyaluronic acid, biotin, probiotics — are worth the difference is a separate question from what the collagen alone costs.

## What each one adds beyond collagen

This is where the two products diverge most.

**Elavate** includes liposomal vitamin C (300mg), hyaluronic acid (120mg), biotin (5mg), probiotics (50 million CFUs), and smaller amounts of zinc, B12, D3 and K2. Of those, vitamin C is the only ingredient that carries an authorised UK/EU claim related to collagen: it contributes to normal collagen formation for the normal function of skin. Having vitamin C in the blend means Elavate can make that claim; whether you'd get it from a separate tablet or your diet instead is a personal choice, not a product question.

**Nutrition Geeks** is collagen peptides and nothing else. Its earlier version included vitamin C and hyaluronic acid, but the brand says both were removed in a reformulation. That means the one authorised collagen-related claim is not available to this product. This isn't a fault — it's a trade-off: a simpler label, a lower price, and a product that assumes you're getting your vitamin C elsewhere.

## What neither of them tells you

Both products leave gaps that a buyer would reasonably want filled.

**Elavate** doesn't state the bag weight or give a definite serving count — "20 to 40 servings" is a range, not a number. It doesn't specify collagen type (I, II or III). The page's claims about clinical proof, joint pain, wrinkles and weight management are not authorised UK/EU health claims, and the text we read cites no study design, size or comparison group. Our [full Elavate label guide](/guides/elavate-collagen-label-dose-price/) covers each claim in detail.

**Nutrition Geeks** says "free from common allergens" without giving an allergen statement, so anyone with a specific allergy should check the physical pack. It doesn't give the proportions of Type I and Type III collagen. The third-party test confirms label accuracy (what's in the bag matches what the label says), which is a quality-control measure, not a test of whether the product works. Our [full Nutrition Geeks label guide](/guides/nutrition-geeks-collagen-dose-price-label/) has the detail.

## Which suits what

Neither is objectively better — they're built for different buyers.

**Elavate suits someone who** wants collagen plus several added actives in one scoop and is prepared to pay a higher per-serving price for the convenience of not buying vitamin C, hyaluronic acid and biotin separately. The multi-collagen blend (bovine, marine, chicken) may also appeal to anyone who wants more than one collagen source, though 98% of the blend is bovine.

**Nutrition Geeks suits someone who** wants a high dose of collagen peptides at the lowest cost per gram, is happy to get vitamin C and any other extras from their diet or separate supplements, and prefers a single-ingredient label with a stated pack weight and serving count.

Neither product has been tested by us, and we make no claim about what either one does.

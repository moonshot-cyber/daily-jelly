Search for collagen in the UK and you'll hit liquid sachets constantly — Absolute Collagen, Zooki, Revive Collagen, Benjamin Button. They're a much bigger, more established category here than jelly sticks are, and it's easy to conflate the two formats since both come in a small single-serve pouch. They're genuinely different products.

## The core difference

A liquid sachet contains a drinkable liquid — you tear it open and drink it, sometimes mixed into water or juice first. A jelly stick contains a soft, set gel — you tear it open and eat it, more like a fruit purée pouch than a drink. Both are single-dose and don't need measuring, but the texture and ritual are different, and so, often, is the dose.

## The dose gap is real

UK liquid sachet brands tend to run at higher collagen doses than the jelly sticks we cover:

- **Absolute Collagen** — 8,000mg hydrolysed marine collagen per sachet, 14 sachets
- **Zooki Marine Collagen** — 5,000mg or 11,000mg per sachet, 14 servings
- **Revive Collagen** — 5,000mg or 8,500mg per sachet, depending on the range
- **Benjamin Button Marine Collagen** — 12,000mg per sachet

Compare that with the jelly sticks in our [buying guide](/where-to-buy): mostly 1,000–2,500mg per stick, with Cloo9 the outlier at 5,000mg. If dose is your main criterion, liquid sachets are currently ahead across the board — though see our [evidence piece](/guides/do-collagen-jelly-sticks-actually-work) on why a bigger number isn't automatically a better result; the most-studied dose (VERISOL, 2.5g) sits well below what most sachets contain.

## Where jelly sticks win

**No liquid, no glugging.** Some people find a thick 30–50ml liquid sachet unpleasant to drink straight — jelly sticks are eaten more like a snack, which some find easier to actually stick with daily.

**Less packaging bulk.** A jelly stick pouch is flatter and smaller than a liquid sachet, which matters if you're travelling or keeping one in a bag.

**The "beauty snack" framing.** This is more about positioning than substance, but jelly sticks are marketed and often taste more like a treat than a supplement — relevant if you've struggled to stick with a liquid or capsule routine because it feels too medicinal.

## Where liquid sachets win

**Availability.** Every major UK pharmacy and health retailer stocks liquid collagen sachets. As we've covered, none currently stock jelly sticks — see [why Boots, Superdrug, and Holland & Barrett don't carry the format](/guides/why-boots-superdrug-holland-barrett-dont-stock-jelly-sticks).

**Dose.** Covered above — if you specifically want a high single dose, sachets currently offer more options at higher doses.

**Track record.** Brands like Absolute Collagen have thousands of UK reviews and years of retail presence. The jelly-stick brands we cover are newer to the UK market, with far less accumulated review history.

## The honest verdict

If you want the highest dose from an established UK brand with next-day availability at a pharmacy, liquid sachets currently win on paper. If you've tried liquid collagen and didn't get on with drinking it, or you're specifically drawn to the Korean "beauty snack" format, jelly sticks are a genuine alternative — just go in knowing the doses are generally lower and the brands newer. Neither format changes whether ingested collagen itself does anything; that's a separate question, covered in our [evidence roundup](/guides/do-collagen-jelly-sticks-actually-work).

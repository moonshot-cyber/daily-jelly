"Inner beauty" is a Korean beauty-retail category that treats ingestible products — jelly sticks, collagen shots, beauty drinks — as part of a skincare routine rather than a separate health-supplement aisle. Walk into a Korean beauty retailer and you'll find a shelf that doesn't really exist in the UK yet: these snacks sit alongside serums and sheet masks rather than in a pharmacy supplements aisle. Jelly sticks, vitamin shots, and single-serve beauty drinks are treated as part of a skincare routine, not a separate health-food category.

## What does "inner beauty" actually mean?

The core idea is simple: skin health is treated as something you influence from the inside as well as the outside. Rather than a supplement you take for general wellness, these products are framed and merchandised as an extension of a skincare routine — bought in the same trip, discussed by the same beauty influencers, and packaged with the same visual language as the serums next to them.

Trade coverage of Korean beauty retail has pointed to sustained growth in this category over the past couple of years, particularly among younger shoppers and international visitors buying Korean beauty products while travelling. Jelly sticks are one of the more visible formats within that shift, alongside collagen shots and beauty-focused teas and powders.

## Why hasn't the inner-beauty trend caught on in the UK yet?

A few structural reasons, not just consumer taste:

**Retail categorisation.** UK supplement retail draws a hard line between "beauty" and "health" sections. Boots, Superdrug, and Holland & Barrett all merchandise collagen as a supplement, next to vitamins — not as a beauty product next to moisturiser. That framing shapes what shoppers expect to find where.

**Format unfamiliarity.** Gummies and powders are the default in UK collagen retail, and consumer habits are sticky. A new format needs a reason to switch, and most UK brands haven't tried to build one yet.

**Import friction.** Getting food-supplement products from Korea into the UK involves customs, labelling, and — depending on the ingredients — Novel Foods considerations that add cost and time. That's a real barrier for smaller brands trying to bring the format over quickly, and it's part of why current UK availability is limited to a handful of specialist importers rather than mainstream retail.

Collagen jelly is now on sale at Target, one of the biggest US retailers. A US-founded brand called Mimitime launched a collagen konjac jelly exclusively in Target's beauty aisle in August 2026, and two jelly sticks we already track show up on Target's website. That's real evidence the format is moving out of K-beauty import shops and into US mass retail. For UK buyers, though, it changes nothing practical yet: Target doesn't trade here, Mimitime has no UK stockist we could find, and the Target-listed sticks are the same products you can already buy through UK routes.

## What actually launched

The launch was covered by [Personal Care Insights](https://www.personalcareinsights.com/news/mimitime-collagen-konjac-jelly-target.html) (18 August 2026), [NetInfluencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/beauty-influencer-ava-lee-teams-with-cocokind-founder-to-launch-konjac-jelly-snack-brand/) (10 August 2026, citing WWD) and [The Dieline](https://thedieline.com/mimitime-brings-konjac-jelly-to-american-shores/) (9 September 2026). Between them, they report:

- **The brand:** Mimitime, co-founded by Priscilla Tsai, who founded the skincare brand Cocokind, and the beauty creator Ava Lee. It's a US brand inspired by Korean products, not a Korean brand exporting to the US. Personal Care Insights says it was developed over two years with Korean suppliers; The Dieline says it is made in Korea.
- **The product:** a konjac jelly pouch with 1,500mg of marine collagen, 30 calories and 3g of sugar per pouch, sweetened with brown sugar. Personal Care Insights and NetInfluencer list four flavours (strawberry, peach, grape, apple); The Dieline lists three.
- **The launch:** exclusive to Target's beauty aisle across the US, from 16 August per NetInfluencer, at $2.99 a pouch. Personal Care Insights reports Target has committed to more than double the brand's shelf space in January 2027.

One sales figure circulated with the launch: an expected US$1 million or more in the first four and a half months, which NetInfluencer attributes to WWD. That's a forecast from the brand's own launch coverage, not a reported result, so we're not reading anything into it.

It's also worth being clear about the format. Mimitime is a **konjac jelly pouch**, not the flat, tear-open collagen jelly stick that brands like BB LAB, HAMCHOROK and Cloo9 sell. Both are collagen in a jelly; they're not the same product shape, and konjac (a plant-based gelling fibre) isn't the base every jelly stick uses.

## Jelly sticks are on Target's website too, as marketplace listings

Searching Target's website also turns up two jelly sticks from the products we track. Both are online listings sold by third parties rather than products Target stocks itself, and neither listing says it's available in store:

- [NOW Foods Collagen Jelly Beauty Complex, Sweet Plum, 10 sticks](https://www.target.com/p/now-foods-collagen-jelly-beauty-complex-sweet-plum-10-ct/-/A-1001855855): $17.68 online, sold by Swanson Health Products. The listing gives 2.5g of hydrolysed bovine collagen (VERISOL) and 2g of sugar per stick.
- [EVERYDAZE Essential Collagen Solution Jelly Stick, Peach, 10 sticks](https://www.target.com/p/everydaze-essential-collagen-solution-jelly-stick-peach/-/A-1002316290): $14.99, sold and shipped by EVERYDAZE. The listing describes a "3000 mg dosage of Vegan Collagen Peptide" per packet. As we explain in our [vegan collagen guide](/guides/vegan-and-plant-based-collagen-jelly-sticks), "vegan collagen" isn't collagen in the animal-protein sense, so read that figure as the brand's description, not a like-for-like collagen dose.

So the honest version of "jelly sticks are in Target" is this: one collagen jelly brand has real shelf space, and the classic jelly stick appears through Target's online marketplace, which is a much smaller step.

Two things stand out. First, Mimitime's 1,500mg is a modest dose, in the same range as the Korean sticks rather than the higher-dose options, and every dose on this table is below or around the amounts used in most clinical trials (see [how much collagen per day](/guides/how-much-collagen-per-day)). Second, Mimitime's single-pouch price is the highest per serving here, which is what you'd expect from a single-serve impulse buy in a beauty aisle rather than a multi-pack.

## Why this doesn't reach UK shelves yet

Target has no UK stores and doesn't sell to UK shoppers in the way a UK retailer does, and we found no UK stockist for Mimitime. The reasons the UK high street doesn't carry jelly sticks, which we covered in [why Boots, Superdrug and Holland & Barrett don't stock them](/guides/why-boots-superdrug-holland-barrett-dont-stock-jelly-sticks), haven't changed because a US retailer moved first.

What the Target launch does show is that a mainstream Western retailer is willing to put a collagen jelly in a beauty aisle, next to other "beauty from within" products. It's the same inner-beauty idea we described in [why Korea is snacking on skincare](/guides/inner-beauty-trend-uk). It isn't the only sign either: Personal Care Insights also reported in September 2026 that Target was adding a holiday range from Clöud Café, a South Korean collagen drinks brand already stocked there. Whether UK retailers follow is a guess, and we're not going to dress it up as a prediction.

Our [Costco guide](/guides/costco-collagen-jelly-sticks-uk-alternative) has the same lesson from another US retailer: a big US listing is a useful signal, but importing a US-retail product into the UK usually costs more than buying the nearest UK-available equivalent.

## What you're actually allowed to claim

Target stocking a product says something about retail demand, not about whether it works. In the UK, the only authorised claim connecting a nutrient to collagen is that **vitamin C contributes to normal collagen formation for the normal function of skin**. For what the evidence on eating collagen does and doesn't show, see [do collagen jelly sticks actually work?](/guides/do-collagen-jelly-sticks-actually-work).

## The honest verdict

Collagen jelly getting its own space in Target's beauty aisle is a genuine milestone for the format in the West, but the product doing it is a US-founded konjac pouch, and the jelly sticks on Target are marketplace listings. For UK buyers, nothing has changed: the options are still importers, direct-to-consumer brands and a handful of UK retailers.

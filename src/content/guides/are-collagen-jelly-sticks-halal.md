This is a real, specific question we saw people searching, and it deserves a properly researched answer rather than a guess — collagen source matters a lot here, and "probably fine" isn't good enough when it comes to dietary compliance. Please treat this as a starting point for your own checking, not a final answer for any specific product.

## Why the collagen source is the whole question

**Marine (fish) collagen** — used in BB LAB, Cloo9, and HAMCHOROK's jelly sticks — is generally considered halal by default, since fish don't require the zabiha (ritual slaughter) process that land animals do. That said, "generally considered halal" isn't the same as certified, and it's still worth checking a product's full ingredient list for anything else in the formulation (gelatin from another source, alcohol-based flavouring, etc.).

**Bovine (cattle-derived) collagen** — used in NOW Foods' Collagen Jelly Beauty Complex (VERISOL) — is a genuinely different question. Halal status depends on whether the source cattle were zabiha-slaughtered. Without third-party certification confirming that, bovine collagen sourcing can't be assumed either way.

**Plant-based "collagen-support" blends** like YIXO's don't raise the same question at all, since there's no animal-derived collagen involved — though as covered in our [ingredients breakdown](/guides/collagen-jelly-stick-benefits-and-ingredients), these aren't collagen in the technical sense either.

## What "halal certified" actually means

A product being halal isn't just about the core ingredient — it's a supply-chain claim, verified by a recognised certification body. In the UK and internationally, the bodies most commonly referenced are **HMC** (Halal Monitoring Committee), **HFA** (Halal Food Authority), **JAKIM** (Malaysia's Department of Islamic Development), and **MUI** (Indonesia's Ulema Council). A product carrying one of these marks has been through an actual audit process; a brand simply saying "halal-friendly" in marketing copy without a certification mark has not.

## What we found checking the six products in our guide

Here's the genuinely useful bit: **BB LAB's manufacturer, Nutrione, does sell halal-certified products** — but they're a separate product line entirely. Their [halal-certified collection](https://bblab.shop/collections/halal) consists of powder-stick products (Good Night Collagen, Low-Molecular Collagen Biotin Plus, The Collagen Powder S Season 2) — not the Pomegranate Collagen S jelly stick we feature. It would be wrong to imply that certification extends to the jelly product just because it's the same manufacturer.

As of publishing, we could not find visible third-party halal certification on any of the six specific jelly-stick products in our [buying guide](/where-to-buy) — BB LAB's Pomegranate Collagen S, FOODOLOGY's Collagen Centella Jelly, Cloo9's Collagen Jelly, YIXO's Vegan Collagen Jelly, NOW Foods' Collagen Jelly Beauty Complex, or HAMCHOROK's Rg+ Pomegranate Collagen Jelly.

## What we'd actually recommend

If halal status matters to your decision, don't take our word for it or a brand's marketing copy — look for an actual certification mark on the product page or packaging, or contact the brand directly and ask which body certified it and for which specific SKU. Marine collagen being generally lower-risk is a reasonable starting assumption, but "generally considered" and "verified" are different things, and this is a case where the difference matters.

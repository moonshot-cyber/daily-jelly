Look at any collagen jelly stick — pink pomegranate pouches, peach flavours, "inner beauty" branding — and the marketing makes a clear assumption about who's buying it. That's a packaging and category choice, not a fact about what's actually in the pouch.

## Where the assumption comes from

Collagen jelly sticks grew out of Korea's "inner beauty" category — a section of the beauty aisle, not the supplements aisle, sitting next to skincare rather than protein powder or joint-care tablets. That placement, and the flavour and colour choices that go with it, is a marketing and retail-category decision. It's also why outreach and coverage in this space (including some of what we've seen ourselves) defaults to a female-beauty-consumer framing without really examining it.

## What's actually gendered: nothing in the formulation

Collagen naturally declines with age — a well-documented, ordinary part of getting older that happens in men and women alike. There's nothing sex-specific about hydrolysed collagen peptides, marine or bovine, that would make them work differently depending on who's eating them. The [evidence we've covered elsewhere](/guides/do-collagen-jelly-sticks-actually-work) on oral collagen doesn't distinguish by sex either — the studies behind it, including the VERISOL research, included specific study populations, but the underlying mechanism (ingested collagen peptides) isn't described anywhere as working differently for men.

To be clear about what we're *not* saying: this isn't a claim that jelly sticks offer men some particular benefit — muscle recovery, joint support, and similar claims go beyond what's authorised, the same as anywhere else on this site. It's simply that the "this is a women's product" framing is a marketing artefact, not a formulation fact.

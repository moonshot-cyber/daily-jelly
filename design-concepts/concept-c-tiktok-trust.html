Concept C

TikTok → Trust

A hooky first screen that meets the audience where the confusion actually starts (the pouch, the "wait, is this the diet one?" moment), then drops straight into clean, high-contrast buy modules. Bold condensed headlines and big product stills for pacing and scroll appeal — not for glow-up energy. No "get glass skin," no urgency countdowns, no meme clutter.

01 — Mood board

High contrast, restrained gloss

Warm blush background, one saturated pink-red for CTAs and headline accents, one green reserved only for "verified" trust badges — never both loud at once. Big rounded cards (jelly-pouch softness) instead of Concept A's sharp plinths. Product photography gets a soft gradient stage and a drop-shadow "held up to camera" feel, not a flat plinth.

04 — UX notes

How the mechanics carry over

CTA hierarchy

A sticky bottom CTA bar on mobile only (44px+ tap target, always the same "See where to buy" action) — desktop keeps CTAs inline per section, no sticky bar, since desktop shoppers don't need the same thumb-reach affordance.

Amazon & affiliate disclosure

Same verbatim text as Concept A, styled as a blush pill-card instead of a stone box — kept equally visible, just re-skinned to match the rounder system. Never placed inside the hook screen itself, to avoid a legal-text tone undercutting the hook.

Email capture

Becomes a rounded gloss-pink card embedded mid-scroll (not a full-bleed band) — matches the "module" pacing of the rest of the page rather than interrupting the story scroll with a colour-inverted band.

"Start here" / journal nav

Kept as "Guides" (no relabel) — this concept's Gen-Z signal comes from pacing and visuals, not from renaming things; over-clever nav labels are exactly the kind of "try-hard" the brief warns against.

05 — TikTok-gen fit, without trying hard

Where the line is, and how this stays on the right side of it

  • Borrow pacing, not vocabulary. Short punchy sections and big product stills read as "made for scrolling" without needing a single piece of slang, meme, or countdown timer — the format itself is the Gen-Z signal, not the copy.
  • The hook is about confusion, not transformation. Opening on "which jelly stick is this?" is inherently un-hypey — it's a clarifying question, not a "your skin will thank you" promise, so the bold visual energy has nothing glow-up-shaped to attach to.
  • One green badge, doing one job. "Verified" and rating badges use the only non-pink accent in the system — deliberately not decorative, so trust signals stay legible against the bolder visual language instead of getting lost in it.
06 — Risks

Where this could go wrong

Trust

The single biggest risk of this whole direction: a bolder, glossier visual system sitting next to a brand mandate of "no glow claims" needs constant copy discipline — every headline needs a second read asking "does the design imply something the text doesn't claim?" Easiest to get wrong on the hook screen specifically.

SEO

All-caps display headings are a CSS text-transform, not literal markup — H1/H2 text content must stay normal-case in the DOM so it doesn't read oddly to screen readers or get mis-weighted; low risk if implemented correctly, worth a code-review flag either way.

Conversion

A persistent sticky CTA is normally a conversion win, but on an affiliate (not first-party checkout) site it can create false urgency mismatch — clicking "See where to buy" repeatedly just to land on a comparison page, not a cart, so copy on that bar needs to set expectations ("Compare 6 UK options" beats a bare "Buy now").

07 — Rebuild cost sketch

Size: M/L

1
Tokens, fonts & radius system

New weight-900 headline treatment, pill radius system across every button/card — touches global.css broadly, more surface area than Concept A's token swap.

2
Hook-screen component

New, unique per page-type (homepage hook differs from article hook) — the most bespoke, highest-effort new component in either concept.

3
Sticky mobile CTA bar

New global component with scroll-position logic — small-to-medium effort, but needs real device testing (notch/safe-area insets, viewport-height quirks on iOS Safari).

4
Content

Zero rewrite for body copy; hook screens need short new headline variants per page (a few words each), not new claims.

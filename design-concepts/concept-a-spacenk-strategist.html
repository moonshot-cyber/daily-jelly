Concept A

SpaceNK × Strategist

Premium editorial shop. Gallery-white space, a serif that reads as a beauty-hall price tag rather than a blog headline, one restrained pomegranate accent, and product photography staged on a plinth rather than floating on a flat background. The thesis: make Daily Jelly feel like the trustworthy shelf edit at a beauty counter, not a comparison blog with pictures bolted on.

01 — Mood board

Calm, warm, editorial

Gallery-white and stone, one deep pomegranate accent used sparingly (never as a background wash), hairline borders instead of shadows, product shots staged on a pale plinth so every jelly pouch reads as "merchandised" rather than "screenshotted."

04 — UX notes

How the mechanics carry over

CTA hierarchy

One solid-ink primary CTA per view ("See where to buy" / "Check on Amazon UK"). Everything else — Stylevana, YesStyle alternates — stays as a plain text link inside the outline-button pattern, so the eye never has two competing loud buttons at once.

Amazon & affiliate disclosure

Kept verbatim, unshortened, in its own stone-background box directly under any Amazon CTA and again in the footer — same placement logic as the live site, just given its own visual weight instead of small grey text.

Email capture

Moved to a full-bleed dark band before the footer on every page type (homepage, article, where-to-buy) — a deliberate "palate cleanser" moment between content and footer, matching SpaceNK's newsletter placement.

"Start here" / journal nav

Renamed "Journal" in the primary nav (replacing "Guides") to read as editorial rather than a blog index — same getCollection-sorted list underneath, just a different label and a plinth-staged thumbnail per entry instead of a text-only card.

05 — TikTok-gen fit, without trying hard

Why this doesn't read as "for your mum"

  • Beauty-retail codes are already Gen-Z-legible. SpaceNK, Cult Beauty, and Space NK's own Instagram feed are exactly what a UK 18–35 beauty-curious shopper already follows — plinth product photography and serif headlines read as "editorial pick," not "your nan's magazine."
  • One accent colour, used correctly, photographs well for screenshots. A pomegranate badge and a clean product plinth crop nicely into an Instagram Story or a WhatsApp share — it doesn't need loud graphics to be shareable.
  • Restraint reads as confidence, not as boring. Skipping gradients/emoji/urgency banners is itself a signal this audience picks up on fast: it looks like a brand that doesn't need to shout, which is more persuasive for a supplement decision than a hype layout would be.
06 — Risks

Where this could go wrong

Trust

Plinth staging on hotlinked (not owned) photography can look slightly "off" if a retailer's source image has visible watermarks or inconsistent lighting — worth a manual pass per product to confirm the plinth crop doesn't clash with the shot's original background.

SEO

None inherent to the visual system — but if "Journal" replaces "Guides" in the nav label, keep the URL path and internal anchor text as "/guides/" and "guide" to avoid unnecessary churn on already-indexed pages.

Conversion

A single low-key CTA per section is a deliberate premium-retail choice, but affiliate sites generally convert better with slightly more repetition of the buy link than a boutique needs — worth A/B-testing CTA frequency before assuming "restrained" wins on clicks, not just on look.
